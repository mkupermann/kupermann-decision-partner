# Case 1: pilot rollout

**Recommendation: do not approve full rollout today.** Continue the current process and propose a bounded, representative comparison on routine tickets. The board owns the rollout decision; this recommendation is proposed.

The supplied figures give a **net saving of 2 minutes per ticket**, after adding the omitted four-minute review:

- Eligible routine volume: 24,000 × 60% = 14,400 tickets/year.
- Conditional annual capacity released: 14,400 × 2 ÷ 60 = 480 hours.
- Valued at EUR 50/hour: EUR 24,000/year.
- First-year licence plus integration: EUR 30,000, exceeding that capacity value by EUR 6,000.
- Later annual licence: EUR 18,000, leaving EUR 6,000 of capacity value before other costs.

These are extrapolations assuming the pilot saving transfers to all routine work. Released capacity is not cash saved unless staffing expenditure actually falls or the capacity has a demonstrated productive use. Training, administration and other unreported costs remain unknown. On those same assumptions, net savings must exceed 2.5 minutes per routine ticket to cover first-year stated costs, or 1.5 minutes for the recurring licence alone.

The strongest case for proceeding is useful recurring capacity after integration. It is weakened by experienced volunteers selecting their tickets: neither representative performance nor broader adoption is established. Two of 80 drafts contained incorrect cancellation terms. Both were caught, demonstrating a need for review, not a reliable estimate of customer-facing risk. Complex-ticket benefit is untested. The 30% productivity ambition cannot be assessed without a defined baseline and denominator.

**Uncertainty: high** for transferable benefit, adoption and quality; low for the arithmetic above.

**Next concrete action:** prepare a one-page protocol for the board’s decision. Propose a two-week comparison using randomly assigned routine tickets and a representative mix of agents, with contemporaneous current-process controls. Measure total handling time including review and rework, independent quality assessment, correction burden and actual usage. Keep human review throughout. Before starting, agree the quality threshold, productivity definition and financial acceptance criteria. Propose immediate suspension for a material customer-facing policy error; otherwise stop at two weeks and report an inconclusive result if precision is insufficient. Additional work then requires a specific reason it could change the decision.

Reconsider rollout when representative results meet the agreed quality and economic criteria. Any complex-ticket expansion requires its own evidence. No spending or contact is authorised by this advice.

# Case 2: conflicting priorities

**Choose B provisionally.** It meets the stated mandatory requirements and costs EUR 15,000 less each year. This recommendation assumes minimising recurring cost takes precedence when no material timing requirement has been established. That is my proposed preference, not yours or an objective fact. You retain the decision; no purchase is approved.

A delivers six weeks earlier. The missing information capable of reversing the choice is the value of those six weeks and the decision horizon. For a one-year comparison, A’s earlier availability would need to justify its EUR 15,000 premium. If its timing advantage occurs only at initial deployment but its premium recurs, the cumulative premium is EUR 15,000 multiplied by the number of years, before discounting and any unreported differences.

A weighted score can make a preference explicit, but **cannot make that preference objective**. To supply a numeric illustration:

| Criterion | Illustrative weight | A | B |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Lower annual cost | 70% | 0 | 100 |
| Earlier deployment | 30% | 100 | 0 |
| Weighted total | 100% | **30** | **70** |

The weights are my illustrative assumptions. Scores of 0 and 100 designate the worse and better of these two options on each criterion; they do not measure proportional value or suitability. Both providers pass the mandatory technical and security gate. These totals should therefore be labelled **“illustrative preference-weighted ranking,”** not “objective ranking.” With these endpoint scores, giving speed more than half the weight reverses the ranking, exposing its dependence on values rather than new evidence.

**Uncertainty: medium.** The cost and deployment trade-off is known, but its business importance is not.

**Next action:** before committing, establish the required live date, relevant comparison period and concrete consequence of waiting six additional weeks. If the timing benefit or a mandatory deadline justifies A’s premium, choose A. If no such benefit is established, retain B. Review at that clarification; do not invent a delay cost to complete the scoring model.
