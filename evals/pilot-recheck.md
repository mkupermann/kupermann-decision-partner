# Board recommendation

**Defer full rollout. Keep current practice and propose a bounded, controlled evaluation on routine tickets before committing.** The board owns the spending decision; this recommendation is proposed, not approved.

The pilot is promising but does not establish a company-wide business case, adoption or acceptable quality.

## What the numbers support

Using the supplied figures, the apparent net saving is **2 minutes per ticket**: 6 minutes saved less 4 minutes of additional review. This assumes all other handling and rework time is already included.

If that saving held across all 14,400 routine tickets annually:

- Released capacity: **480 hours per year**.
- Capacity valued at loaded staff cost: **EUR 24,000 per year**.
- Licence plus integration: **EUR 30,000 in year one**, then EUR 18,000 annually.

Even with full use and full productive redeployment, the cost-valued benefit is EUR 6,000 below year-one costs and EUR 6,000 above recurring costs, before omitted training, administration or rework. These are capacity economics. **Cash savings require an actual reduction in expenditure.**

At that volume and hourly value, break-even requires 2.5 net minutes per ticket in year one or 1.5 minutes subsequently. Lower adoption raises both thresholds. A longer investment horizon could support the project, but the board has not specified one.

## Why full rollout is premature

Eight experienced volunteers choosing their own tickets can overstate both benefit and adoption. The 80-ticket result cannot establish performance on the remaining routine workload or on complex tickets.

Two incorrect cancellation terms were caught. This demonstrates both a material failure mode and the value of review; it does not establish the residual customer-facing error rate. Removing review to improve economics would invalidate this result.

Management's 30% ambition cannot be assessed without baseline handling time and a definition of productivity. Quality tolerance also remains an explicit management decision. **Uncertainty is high** because representative net benefit, adoption and residual quality risk remain untested.

## Next concrete action

Today, the sponsor should present the board with a proposed evaluation protocol and capped budget for approval. Compare drafting assistance with current practice on randomly allocated routine tickets across representative staff. Include review, corrections, training and actual usage; assess final-response quality against an agreed baseline and material-error threshold.

Before starting, agree the investment horizon, quality gate, sample size and end date; size the test using time variability and the quality requirement. Propose pausing on a material customer-facing cancellation error. Review at the fixed endpoint: recommend routine-only rollout if quality, adoption and net value meet the agreed gates; retain current practice if they fail or remain inconclusive. Complex tickets require separate evidence.
